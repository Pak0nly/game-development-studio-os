How the look is made: procedural Cycles materials (noise, Voronoi cracks,
per-block colour variation, bevel-node edge highlights on upper edges, local
ambient occlusion, pseudo-script and seal-sigil masks generated with numpy)
are baked into plain PBR maps; the engines only see standard textures.

## Decisions taken inside the order (for the Director to check)

1. **Dig light.** A point light beside the lava reaches the tunnel only at
   grazing angles, so the dig did nothing visible (checked in Eevee and Cycles).
   Added `L_Lava_Spill_East`: a 90 deg spot above the lava aimed through the
   removable wall, which shadows it completely until t = 4 s. The lava channel
   was placed in cells X 16..18, 2 m from that wall, for the same reason.
2. **Readability fill.** A dim neutral directional light from above (soft
   shadow) plus a near-black ambient, so walls and rock read in the game view.
   It is listed like every other light, so both engines get it.
3. **Extra lights.** One dim torch in the upper corridor and a faint ember
   glow inside the stair shaft, so the breakthrough reads from above. The work
   order named only the two chamber sconces.
4. **Fourth script panel** in the upper corridor; three are in the chamber.
5. **PBR, not ramp shading.** The kit uses standard metallic/roughness
   materials, because the brief's "stylised lit" ramp would need custom
   shaders in each engine and would make the comparison about shader porting.

## What falls short (honest)

- **The figure is a blockout, not a character.** It is built from a skin
  modifier plus blended ellipsoids, then voxel-remeshed and decimated. It reads
  well at game distance and is fine for judging light on a character (glowing
  seams, glossy glass horns, brass rings, wax). Up close it falls short: the
  face is minimal (no mouth, brow and cheek bumps only), the hands are simple
  claws, the feet are stubs, the limbs are tube-like, and the loincloth is a
  stiff flap. No rig.
- **The contract script is procedural pseudo-handwriting.** It reads as
  glowing writing on the walls, but the glyphs are scribbles rather than
  designed letters.
- **The floor joint grid is regular.** The stones inside each 2 m tile are
  irregular, but the tile borders form straight glowing lines across the room.
  That is game-readable but more regular than a hand-laid floor.
- **Lava is a scrolling texture with a mild crust pattern**, not a flow
  shader. Its own glow lights nothing in Eevee or URP; the lights carry that.
  Unreal's Lumen will add emissive bounce, which is an engine difference.
- **The rock mass** is one tiled material (4 m repeat) on a noise-displaced
  mesh. It is good at game distance; up close the tiling and the Voronoi
  fracture lines are visible.
- **Unity and Unreal axis mappings are derived, not tested** (no engine was
  allowed). The layout lists pre-converted values, and `UR_AxisMarker.fbx` is
  included so stage 2 can verify the mapping once before building the scene.
- **Physical light values follow a stated convention** (the Blender glTF
  "standard" one). Only the ratios are binding; the absolute level is matched
  through exposure, as SCENE_SPEC section 8 allows.
- **The Eevee preview is a kit check, not a target look.** Each engine's GI,
  reflections and tonemapper will differ.
- **Texture volume is high**: 160 MB of PNGs in LFS, since most sets are 2048
  and the separate roughness/metallic maps duplicate the packed ones. Stage 2
  can drop the unpacked copies or downsize if import time matters.
- The edge and occlusion masks were baked at 48 samples; faint bake noise is
  possible at very close range. The removable wall shares wall A's textures, so
  its tunnel-facing back has lower texel density (it faces away from both cameras).
