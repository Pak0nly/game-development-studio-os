# Underreign — Game Concept

> **Status:** Concept v1, approved by the Human Developer on 2026-11-09 with the
> answers recorded in [`DISCOVERY_DECISIONS.md`](DISCOVERY_DECISIONS.md)
> (UD-01 to UD-33). Numbers, names of creatures and tuning remain proposals
> until a later Bible or Work Order fixes them. This document authorises no
> implementation by itself.

Underreign is an original game in the dungeon-management genre. Only genre
mechanics are used as a base (digging, building rooms, attracting and keeping
creatures, defending against intruders). No names, creatures, characters, room
names, levels, art, music, UI look or text of any existing game are copied
(DEC-041).

## 1. World and tone

Below the bright surface kingdoms lies the Deep: stacked strata of soil, stone,
old ruins, flooded caves, crystal and magma. Each stratum is older, richer and
more dangerous than the one above. The player is the Heart of an underground
people that wants to take a slice of the Deep for itself.

Threats come from two sides: surface expeditions dig down for loot and glory,
and things that slept in the lowest strata wake when the player digs too deep
(UD-06; the first prototype has threats from above only).

Tone: dark fantasy with dry, mischievous humour (UD-05). Proposal: the world is grim, the
narrating voice smirks. No parody.

| Stratum | Character |
|---|---|
| Surface | where the expeditions come from |
| Soil | fast to dig, collapses easily |
| Stone and old ruins | solid, with finds |
| Flooded caves | water the player can use |
| Crystal | valuable, attracts strong creatures |
| Magma and the Sleeper | riches and the threat from below |

## 2. Playable peoples

Five peoples at release, the Crystal Brood later (UD-07, UD-32, UD-33). Each people has its
own Heart, workers, creatures, rooms (same functions, own look), trap family
and one vertical strength. Peoples also meet each other as rivals in campaign
and free play later; the prototype has expeditions only (UD-09).

### Ashen Pact — fire and contracts

- **Play:** proud fire beings who settle everything by contract. The classic
  "evil" flavour, but only one people among several.
- **Traps:** fire, heat, explosions. **Vertical strength:** heat rises; a fire
  on a lower layer harms the layer above.
- **Look:** tall, lean figures whose skin looks like cooling lava: a black,
  cracked crust with orange glow in the seams that brightens when they are
  angry or working. They wear horns of black glass with embers glowing inside,
  one ring around the horn per contract signed (UD-28); there is no ember
  crown. Clothing is sparse: brass chains, wax-sealed contract scrolls hanging
  from belts, red wax seals pressed into the skin as rank marks. Eyes like
  coals.
- **Workers (cinderlings):** knee-high soot figures with a glowing ember in the
  chest, leaving ash footprints.
- **Architecture:** black basalt, brass fittings, red wax seals on doors, walls
  carved with glowing contract script; floors warm and slightly glowing.
- **Palette:** black, ember orange, brass, wax red.

### Mycelid Choir — fungi, one voice

- **Play:** a patient fungal colony that speaks with a single shared voice.
  Slow, but spreads everywhere.
- **Traps:** living traps that grow, spread and must be fed. **Vertical
  strength:** roots and spores grow through floors and ceilings.
- **Look:** figures of stacked mushroom caps and stalks, from hunched to
  towering; glowing gills under the caps; fine white threads trail behind them
  and connect them to the ground. They hum in chorus.
- **Workers (sporelings):** small round puffballs on root feet.
- **Architecture:** rooms overgrown with mycelium mats, cap-shaped roofs,
  bioluminescent blue-green light, spore haze.
- **Palette:** bone white, moss green, bioluminescent teal, rot brown.

### Drowned Court — water and a sunken city

- **Play:** proud exiles of a sunken city who keep court ceremony even under
  water.
- **Traps:** floods, currents, pressure locks. **Vertical strength:** store
  water high and release it downward at the right moment.
- **Look:** a deep-sea people from eternal darkness (UD-30): almost
  transparent skin through which glowing veins show, a luminous lure on the
  forehead like an anglerfish, too many eyes, long thin limbs. Eerie, but they
  keep court ceremony: salt-crusted court robes that never dry, coral crowns
  and mother-of-pearl jewellery. Water always drips from them.
- **Workers (tide-hands):** small hermit-crab-like servants in shell armour.
- **Architecture:** a sunken palace: barnacled marble columns, shell mosaics,
  puddles and shallow pools, small waterfalls between floors.
- **Palette:** deep teal, pearl white, coral pink, tarnished silver.

### Gearbound Kin — gears and tinkering

- **Play:** small exiled kobold tinkerers, clever and fussy, who love mechanics.
- **Traps:** mechanical traps with the best wiring. **Vertical strength:**
  lifts, shafts, moving floors, trapdoors.
- **Look:** folklore kobolds (UD-29), mammal-like, not reptilian: small,
  wiry, with very large ears. Soot-stained leather aprons, magnifying goggles,
  many tool belts, and gear patched together from scrap, including the
  occasional mechanical arm or leg they built themselves. Hair singed at the
  tips.
- **Workers (rivet-drones):** walking boxes on four spider legs with a ticking
  wind-up spring on top.
- **Architecture:** pipes, gears, steam vents, chain-and-counterweight lifts,
  brass and oak, warning stripes.
- **Palette:** copper, oak brown, soot black, warning yellow.

### Deep Dwarves — stone and weight

- **Play:** a people that has lived under the earth for centuries and never
  saw the surface (UD-32). They dig fastest and build the strongest floors,
  ceilings and walls, so they are the people for the player's goal of making
  the realm breach-proof from above and below (UD-11). As rivals they dig in
  from the side in campaign and free play.
- **Traps (proposal):** stone and weight: collapsing vaults, sealing walls,
  crushing blocks, rolling boulders. **Vertical strength:** the strongest
  structure; reinforced floors and ceilings that hold, and controlled collapses
  that bury what is above or below.
- **Look:** pale, almost colourless skin and large, light-sensitive eyes
  (they squint or wear stone visors in bright light). Beards braided with stone
  beads and ore rings. Armour of rock that has grown fast to them, with crystal
  and ore veins showing at the seams. They must stay clearly different from the
  surface dwarves of Hearthlands: no shared look, model or asset.
- **Workers (proposal, name open):** stocky diggers with stone-plated backs and
  oversized hands, working in pairs.
- **Architecture:** carved straight out of the rock: massive pillars, heavy
  stone doors, low vaulted halls, ore veins lit by dim lanterns.
- **Palette (proposal):** slate grey, pale bone, ore bronze, dim lantern amber.

### Crystal Brood — later

- **Play:** an insect-like hive that makes crystal sing; grows through the
  deepest strata.
- **Traps:** resonance, light, shattering crystal.
- **Look:** insect bodies with translucent crystal carapaces that break light
  into colours; they communicate by ringing tones. Hives of hexagonal crystal
  cells.

Creature names are illustrative proposals: Ashen Pact — brand-hound,
oathkeeper, ember-witch; Mycelid Choir — puffcap, rotwarden, spore-matron;
Drowned Court — eel-knight, brine-oracle, wreck-crab; Gearbound Kin —
boltwright, steam-golem, gyrocopter scout.

## 3. Core loop

1. **Dig:** mark rock; workers dig and claim the ground. Rock is harder deeper
   down.
2. **Build rooms by function:** Nest (sleep), Pantry (food), Drill Hall
   (training), Archive (research), Forge (traps and doors), Hoard (gold), Cells
   (prisoners), Shrine (healing). Room size and neighbours matter.
3. **Attract and keep creatures:** a Call-stone on claimed ground attracts
   creatures; which ones come depends on rooms, depth and people. Creatures want
   pay, food, sleep and the right neighbours; unhappy ones sulk, fight or leave.
4. **Defend:** expeditions arrive in waves from the surface, later also threats
   from below. Traps, doors, creatures and the layout decide the fight.
5. **Grow deeper:** richer strata, better creatures, bigger risk.

The player does not take direct control of a creature for now (UD-18).

## 4. Trap systems

- **Wiring (core, UD-14):** traps are assembled from Triggers (pressure plate,
  tripwire, sight crystal, weight on a lift), Links (chains, pipes, spore
  threads) and Effects (blade, flood, fire, cage, collapse). One trigger can fire
  several effects, also on other layers. Ready-made traps exist for players
  who do not want to tinker.
- **Vertical traps:** trapdoors dropping intruders one layer down (into Cells, a
  flooded pit or a creature den), falling ceilings, boulders down a shaft,
  murder holes shooting into the room below.
- **Environment as a trap:** release stored water downward, let gas sink, let
  fire spread, collapse a weak ceiling.
- **Lures and decoys (UD-16, not in the prototype):** fake hoards, a false
  Heart, illusions. Intruders have greed, fear and courage.
- **Intruders learn (UD-15):** a survivor tells the next wave; known traps
  become weaker, so the player rebuilds.
- **Upkeep:** workers re-arm traps; living traps need feeding.

## 5. Vertical layers

- A map has three to five layers (UD-10), connected by stairs, ramps, shafts and
  lifts.
- Each layer has its own rock and riches (see the strata table).
- Gravity applies to water, gas, heat and collapse in the full game; the
  prototype uses heat rising only (UD-24, supersedes the prototype part of
  UD-12).
- The player decides where intruders get in: guard the entrances, or let them
  in and drop them.
- **View (UD-11):** play focuses mostly on one layer: that layer is sharp, the
  layers above are cut away, the layer below shows through faintly. One key
  steps up or down; a side view helps with shafts.
- **Breach-proofing (UD-11, Human note):** the player must later be able to
  secure the realm against break-ins from below as well as from above, for
  example with reinforced floors and ceilings, sealed shafts, listening posts
  that hear digging, and counter-tunnels. Intruders and rivals may dig in from
  any side, so every layer boundary is a possible front.

## 6. Unique selling points

1. A truly vertical dungeon where water, gas, heat and collapse follow gravity.
2. Several peoples that play differently, not reskins.
3. Trap wiring across layers: players build their own trap machines.
4. Squeezed from two sides: the surface above and the waking Deep below.
5. Intruders who remember and adapt.
6. A realm that has to be secured in all directions, not only along a front
   line.

## 7. Release scope (direction)

- Platform: PC (Windows, Steam) first (UD-03).
- Engine: Unity, same version as Hearthlands, 6000.6.4f1 (UD-04).
- Look: stylised 3D models with clear colours, slightly oblique top-down
  (UD-19); one sample first, then the Human decides.
- Modes: one campaign per people and free play against the computer;
  multiplayer decided later (UD-20).

## 8. First playable prototype (UD-21, UD-22)

Goal: prove that digging across layers plus wired traps is fun. Placeholder
(greybox) graphics; no art decisions.

- One people: Ashen Pact (UD-08).
- One map, three layers, about 20 minutes.
- Five rooms: Nest, Pantry, Drill Hall, Forge, Hoard; one Call-stone.
- Four creatures with pay, food, sleep and mood.
- Six trap parts: two triggers, three effects and one trapdoor, simply wired.
- One gravity rule: heat rises instead of water (UD-24), the Ashen Pact's own
  vertical strength.
- Three intruder types (scout, brute, healer) in three waves; they remember
  one trap.
- Win: survive the waves. Lose: the Heart is destroyed.
