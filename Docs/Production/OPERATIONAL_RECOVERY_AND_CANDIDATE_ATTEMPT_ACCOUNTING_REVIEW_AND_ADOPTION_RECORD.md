## Evaluation

### Problem and recurrence

The problem is cross-project and recurring: process-count contracts were
used as terminal candidate budgets even when Unity only compiled a new Editor
script, never dispatched the task entry point, rejected its own task-owned
status/log files or produced invalid evidence before a candidate existed.
This created repeated governance churn without evaluating the intended work.

### Alternatives considered

- **Keep raw process caps:** rejected because it turns infrastructure noise
  into false candidate conclusions and unnecessary Human checkpoints.
- **Permit unlimited retries:** rejected because it weakens accountability and
  enables blind loops.
- **Remove fail-closed protection:** rejected because authority, protected
  sources and uncertain output still require hard stops.
- **Separate bounded accounting classes:** adopted because it preserves
  protection while allowing cause-specific recovery before a candidate
  attempt begins.

### Adopted design

1. Planned setup, operational recovery, candidate attempts and formal
   creative corrections are separate classes.
2. A candidate attempt begins only at an unambiguous attempt-start event
   after authority, identity, protected-source, process/lock and output guards
   pass.
3. Pre-attempt operational errors do not consume candidate or creative
   correction budgets when they produce no candidate-affecting mutation and
   satisfy exact recovery predicates.
4. The default bounded task has at most two operational recovery actions;
   planned setup does not consume them.
5. Every recovery needs an identified cause, one changed condition, exact
   source/candidate state and clean process/lock state.
6. Work Orders must separate allowances and task-owned output namespaces.
7. Recovery stops on protected drift, uncertain ownership, failed
   restoration, scope/method change, unchanged repetition or exhausted
   allowance.
